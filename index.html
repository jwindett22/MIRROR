How the trip to campus is modeled

About MIRROR

MIRROR plays a full week at UNC Charlotte, 24 hours a day, from the real Fall 2026 class schedule and public data. Every student, employee, resident, visitor and fan is a synthetic agent with a reason to move: a class, an office, lunch, the gym, the library, a game. Cars enter from seven approaches on the real road network, pick a gate and a deck or lot, queue at real signals, stop signs and left turns, and leave; buses, trains, walkers, cyclists and paratransit are on the map; weather comes from the National Weather Service forecast, incidents from NCDOT DriveNC, crowd events from the football and basketball schedules and the PaTS game-day plan. Buildings color by how full their rooms are; the number at the top of the Campus tab is everyone on campus right now. Pick a View to see one question at a time, hover anything for its numbers, and use the Dials tab for scenarios. Road speeds were calibrated against TomTom observations on Nov 12 and 14, 2026 (see Roads and bottlenecks); people counts, capacities and signal timings are still model outputs and stand-ins until the measured data on the asks list arrives.

Who comes, and when

Everyone on foot follows shortest paths on the OpenStreetMap footway, cycleway and minor-road network. Between classes, a student with 15 minutes or more picks one thing to do or nothing: a meal (likeliest at lunch and dinner hours), coffee at one of six shops (morning-heavy, sometimes followed by the library or a lounge), Atkins Library or a Student Union or Cone lounge (likelier with longer gaps), or UREC or Belk Gym (gaps over 75 minutes). After the last class, commuters stay about a third of the time to study, work out or eat before leaving, residents more often. Each venue is chosen by seats and walking distance. The shares are dials; hours come from Google listings where found.

The schedule gives every class meeting with its enrollment, room and time, but not which student sits in which seat. The page builds synthetic students: seats in each class are filled first by students already on campus whose last class ended (the "same student" dial), the rest by students arriving for their first class. Each student's first class sets the arrival, the last class sets the departure. Residents are the share that does not commute; they are assigned to the residence halls by floor area and walk from there. A dialed share of commuters live in the apartment complexes next to campus (OpenStreetMap apartment buildings clustered by floor area): they walk in (55%), drive the short trip from the complex (30%) or ride CATS to the Mary Alexander or JW Clay stop (15%). The rest arrive from farther away by the mode dials.

Mode and destination

Drivers pick a lot or deck by a gravity rule: capacity, discounted by walking distance to the first building, among facilities with space, with surface lots weighted ahead of decks so they fill first; full lots send cars to the next choice and the overflow count is shown. Residents' cars (a dialed share, 45% default) occupy the resident lots and decks all day, with a fifth making one trip out and back. Rail riders alight at UNC Charlotte–Main, or at JW Clay for the CRI buildings, on the next train before class. Bus riders from farther away arrive on CATS at the Mary Alexander or JW Clay stop; people who walk or bike in from beyond the map enter where the sidewalk network reaches its edge. Niner Transit carries transfers: when the walk from a station or deck to the building is over 650 m and a loop serves both ends, most riders take the loop. Nobody appears at a building out of nowhere: every arrival starts at a deck, a station, a bus stop, a residence hall, a nearby complex or a walk-in point. Faculty and staff are 4,212 employees (IPEDS 2024–25). Staff may work up to two days a week remotely but never both Friday and Monday; the share who do and the number of days are dials, remote days are drawn with Friday and Monday most wanted, and the same person keeps the same remote days across the week. Instructors are built from the day's sections, chained by subject so one person teaches several, arriving 20 to 85 minutes before their first class and leaving after their last (full-time, 70%, stay to late afternoon). Staff go to named university buildings weighted by floor area, arriving around 8 and leaving around 5, with a 6 a.m. shift for 15%. Employees drive 88%, ride 7% and walk 5%, park in faculty/staff decks and lots first (student decks as a last resort), and a third walk to lunch.

Signal timing testbed

Charlotte DOT operates the signals around campus; the splits and coordination plans live in its controllers and are not published. The testbed stands in: a dialed peak cycle (120 s default) on the University City and N Tryon corridors, 80% of that midday, 90 s evenings and 70 s late; a main-street green share per signal; actuated pedestrian phases with 7 s walk plus clearance by crossing width (26–30 s on six-lane University City, 14–20 s on side streets); the three pedestrian hybrid beacons dark until pressed. Two what-ifs: put the HAWK beacons and the pedestrian phases on recall during the 10 minutes after each class change, so crowds are not waiting on a push button. The Cameron signal (10-2151) uses the real phase structure and clearance intervals from its 2015 CDOT design sheet. Replacing the assumed splits with CDOT timing sheets is the data fix; the request is in the sources note.

Getting around with a disability

Ground and walking time. Ground elevation comes from the USGS 3D Elevation Program's bare-earth lidar, sampled every metre along every walkable line on the map. Walking speed on a slope follows Tobler's function: 80 m per minute on the level, about 16% slower up a 5% grade and 40% slower up a 15% grade, slightly faster down a gentle slope and slower again down a steep one. Stairs are walked at 30 m per minute going up and 37 coming down, measured along the ground. Routes are chosen on time, not distance, so a longer level path can beat a short flight of steps. Each route's time is the average of walking it in the two directions; the uphill and downhill times are kept separately but trips do not use them yet. The Terrain and stairs layer shows the relief, a contour every 5 m, the 183 stair segments OpenStreetMap has, and the stretches the lidar says are too steep to be a plain path and are not mapped as stairs. Those are probable steps and need a site check. Stairs inside buildings, under roofs and on bridges cannot be seen by the lidar.

The Access layer shows every stair segment OpenStreetMap has on campus, the three elevators and eleven kerbs it tags, the ADA and HC lots, and the Niner Paratransit hubs Passio GO publishes. A dialed share of students use a wheelchair or mobility device: they move at 60 m per minute on step-free routes, and when the step-free route between classes is over 700 m or cannot be covered in the gap (for the half assumed registered with Paratransit), or does not exist at all, the trip becomes a scheduled Niner Paratransit ride (weekdays 7 a.m. to midnight, campus only, rides booked 24 hours ahead take priority). The van simulation reports waits and late drop-offs for the number of vans on the dial. Blue Line stations have level boarding and Niner Transit buses are accessible, so rail and bus arrivals are treated as step-free to the platform or stop; the walk from there is routed step-free.

Roads and bottlenecks

Each car enters from one of seven approaches (shares illustrative) and takes the quickest of its shortest path and the five gate routes (main entrance at Mary Alexander, Broadrick, Cameron, John Kirk at 49, John Kirk at Van Landingham), judged on congested time at departure; it then drives to the real deck or lot entrance. Travel time is free flow plus signal delay, a protected left-turn phase with its own queue, link delay as segments fill (lane-based capacity with the signal green share on the arterials), stop signs on minor approaches, and yielding at unsignalized crosswalks and driveways; through traffic on 49, 29, Harris and Mallard Creek is placed first from the NCDOT counts and is slowed by the same rules. Calibrated Nov 16, 2026 against TomTom speeds on Nov 12 (class day) and Nov 14 (game day): arterial free speed 45 mph where OSM has no limit tagged, signal oversaturation delay at HCM scale (1 min per unit v/c over 0.8, was 10), through traffic shaped only by daylight camera hours; speed error on 10 segments × 17 hours fell from 23 to 14 points of free-flow speed. Road load counts cars on each segment per 15 minutes against an illustrative capacity by road class; entrance and deck-gate load compare cars per 5 minutes with an assumed throughput. Campus-bound cars are what the schedule implies under the dials; on top of them the public roads carry through traffic from NCDOT's 15-minute tube counts on school days: NC 49 in three segments (31,290 a day at the main entrance, 31,268 at John Kirk, 45,187 east of Mallard Creek Church), US 29 in two (21,741 north of NC 49, 30,365 south of Mallard Creek Church), Mallard Creek Church Rd (10,693) and John Kirk Dr (14,950), each by direction and quarter hour; Harris Blvd uses its AADT with the NC 49 profile. The Utilities layer shows Duke Energy's 100 kV transmission lines and the substation that serves campus (HIFLD), the four regional utility plants, and the City of Charlotte storm-pipe network with diameters; the pipe structures' ground elevations give the sidewalk grades used for step-free routing. Traffic signals come from the NCDOT statewide signal inventory (twenty on the public roads around campus, three of them pedestrian hybrid beacons on University City Boulevard); each signal on a car's route adds the delay computed by the signal timing testbed (cycle length and main-street green share by time of day, corridor coordination; Cameron's phasing and clearances from the 2015 CDOT design sheet, the rest assumed). Crosswalks are the points where the OpenStreetMap sidewalk network meets a road; the page counts the modeled people and cars meeting at each one every quarter hour.

Week, weekends, weather, events

The clock runs midnight to midnight and rolls through the week. Weekdays come from the class schedule; weekends have no classes, a small staff presence, residents going to meals, the rec center and the library, commuter visitors, and through traffic scaled from the weekday counts. The weather for each date comes from the NWS hourly forecast (or a forced scenario) and changes mode shares, walking speed, road capacity, runners on the greenway, lots in the floodplain and the size of a game crowd. Crowd events come from the football and basketball home schedules; a football day follows the PaTS plan: permit decks and day-of parking, two open gates, Phillips Road closed, the Football North and South shuttles, police-directed exits, and tailgating at the 49th Acre, Hauser Pavilion and the lots by the stadium. Incidents on the approaches come from NCDOT DriveNC when the data file is refreshed, or from the crash scenario on the Dials tab.

Parking supply used by the model

Deck capacities: UNC Charlotte Parking and Transportation Services student-lot count report, Spring 2014 (the most recent public space counts found); Union Deck adds the 627 spaces of the 2018 expansion. Surface lots are sized from their county and OpenStreetMap footprints at 28 m² a space (Lot 25's footprint gives 497 by that rule, matching its 2014 count) and assigned to resident or commuter permits by where they sit; the lot names follow the PaTS map where a name is in OpenStreetMap and are guesses otherwise. Cone Deck and East Deck 1 are faculty/staff decks with no public count found and are estimates. OpenStreetMap labels West Deck as Central Deck; the match is by location and is flagged. Built-in assumptions with no dial

  • Signal timing: cycle lengths, green splits and walk phases are assumed for every signal; no timing sheets from CDOT, NCDOT or the university.
  • Drivers at unsignalized crosswalks wait for walkers for at most 45 seconds, then edge through; one more car follows through the same gap. A queue that fills the road back to the junction behind it holds cars at that junction.
  • A walker needs 10 seconds to clear a crossing; a queued car takes 7 m of road and cars discharge 2 seconds apart per lane.
  • Surface-lot capacities come from each lot's paved area, not a space count. Deck capacities are from Parking and Transportation Services.
  • Visitor-area sizes inside the decks (Union 300, CRI 190, South Village 180, Cone 180, East Deck 1 200) are assumed.
  • Which entrance each permit type uses at each deck is from the PaTS rules; the share of traffic using each entrance is assumed.
  • Honeywell Innovation Hub site: the closures are from the Facilities Management and PaTS notices; the fence line on the map is approximate.
  • Roofs of buildings finished after the 2016 lidar flight are modelled by hand from photos and aerials; several heights are assumed.
  • Walking paths inside campus are drawn as brick; roadside sidewalks and wooded paths only where seen.
  • Bikes are held to the scooter no-ride areas as well as the wheels-free core; bikes and scooters do not wait at signals.
  • No weekday visitor count exists; the Weekday visitors dial starts at an assumed 1,200 cars a day.
